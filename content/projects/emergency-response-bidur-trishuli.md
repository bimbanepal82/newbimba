The third initiative emerged from a very different circumstance.

When communities in the Rasuwa and Trishuli/Bhotekoshi area were affected by a flash flood/debris flood following the fall of glacier/ice mass in the high Himalayan region around Rasuwa, immediate humanitarian and health needs arose.

Among those affected were older people who had lost medicines that they regularly depended upon. For someone living with a chronic health condition, losing regular medication during a disaster can interrupt treatment and create additional health risks.

BIMBA Nepal responded together with partners. **BIMBA Nepal, Geriatric Society of Nepal and Art of Living Nepal** jointly organized a free health camp on **Bhadra 20, 2083**, at the holding center of **Ranabhumaneshwar School, Sirkhali, Bidur Municipality–3, Nuwakot**.

This became BIMBA Nepal's **Emergency Response Project**.

## A health response during crisis

The emergency response provided a combination of medical and psychosocial services, and particularly considered the needs of older people affected by the disaster.

### Services included

- Geriatric consultation
- General medical examination
- Orthopedic services
- Cardiac assessment
- Dental services
- Osteopathy services
- Trauma relief
- Stress management
- Psychosocial counselling
- Medicines
- Health materials

The consequences of a disaster extend beyond physical injury. Floods and displacement can bring:

- Loss of medicines
- Interruption of treatment
- Physical injury
- Displacement
- Fear and uncertainty
- Loss of property
- Emotional stress
- Disruption of family and community life

An effective health response therefore needs to consider the person as a whole.

## Working through partnership

The program was jointly organized by BIMBA Nepal, Geriatric Society of Nepal and Art of Living Nepal.

The health response also received important support through **Nepal APF Hospital and the Armed Police Force, Balambu**, including medical professionals deployed locally who contributed to examination and treatment services.

This reflects an important part of BIMBA Nepal's approach: **we do not work alone. We work with people and organizations that can bring knowledge, resources, expertise and compassion together.**

## Beyond relief

Emergency response is often associated with food, shelter and physical relief. These are important. But people affected by disasters can also lose access to medicines, healthcare and emotional security.

For BIMBA Nepal: **relief is not only about what we give. It is also about how we stand with people.**

> A wound needs treatment.
>
> A lost medicine needs replacement.
>
> A health problem needs attention.
>
> And a person facing a crisis needs human support.

This is why medical care, psychosocial support and human connection were all considered within the response.
